The Agency, five ways.

Five versions of concept 1, The Agency, now carrying the peanut from the site favicon. Every one keeps the compass star, the peanut and the field-operations voice. They differ in how official, how tactical or how modern they feel. Colors and fonts are unchanged from brand guide v1. Each version is shown full size on the pages that follow, along with a one-color version and a favicon-size test.

Concept 1A · Round 3

Crest Heraldic

The Agency seal with a heraldic shield inside the ring. The shield's top band carries three peanuts, and the compass star and peanut roundel sit below. It's the most "official" of the five, like a coat of arms for a department that shouldn't exist.

Where it lives: Hoodie back, embroidered patches, certificates, the About page.

Concept 1B · Round 3

Stars & Motto Ceremonial

A ring of 16 stars around the compass star, and a ribbon reading SEMPER SUBTERRA ("always underground"). That's where peanuts grow and where good operatives stay. "Peanut Division" moves onto the ring.

Where it lives: Main seal on packaging, merch chest prints, the tester-set box.

Concept 1C · Round 3

Tactical Patch Field issue

A subdued olive patch with a stitched edge and crosshairs locked on a peanut. "Field Ops / Peanut Div." It looks like it came off a jacket sleeve. It's the most merch-ready of the five: it would make a great real embroidered patch or hat.

Where it lives: Hats, velcro patches, a jacket or hoodie sleeve, stickers.

Concept 1D · Round 3

Monoline Modern agency

The same seal redrawn with thin single-weight lines, and gold only on the peanut. It's how a government agency looks after a pricey rebrand: calm, precise, a little too clean. It holds up best on the website and in print.

Where it lives: Site header and footer, letterhead, business cards, the brand guide cover.

Concept 1E · Round 3

Credentials Field agent

A gold field-agent badge, the kind that gets flashed at a door, with CROP OF NOW on the top banner and FIELD AGENT on the bottom. The compass seal sits in the middle. It's the funniest in person, and it could be a real enamel pin.

Where it lives: Enamel pins, keychains, the "Authorized Personnel" merch line, social avatar.
